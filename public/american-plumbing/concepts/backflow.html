The requirement

What Louisiana requires of a backflow device

A backflow prevention device keeps water from your property's plumbing from flowing back into the public supply. Because a device can wear or stick without any sign, the state asks for a certified test every year, and the water system expects the result on file.

"Louisiana law requires every property with a backflow prevention device to have it tested and inspected once a year by a properly certified plumber."

    Why it matters

    What causes backflow

    Backflow is water running the wrong way, from a property's plumbing back toward the supply everyone drinks from. It usually starts with a change in pressure. These are the five causes we see.

    1. Broken water mainsA break drops pressure on the supply side and can pull water back out of a property.
    2. Extended power lossPumps that hold pressure steady can stop, and pressure in the system falls.
    3. Plumbing overhaulsDraining or shutting down lines during major work changes pressure throughout the system.
    4. Emergency water consumptionHeavy draw on the main, such as firefighting, can lower pressure for everyone nearby.
    5. Dramatic pressure changeAny sudden swing can reverse the flow where a device isn't working as it should.

    Repairs and installs

    When a device fails the test

    A failed test doesn't mean starting over with someone else. The same certified plumber repairs the assembly, or installs a new one when it can't be repaired or the property needs one for the first time.

    • Repair of backflow assembliesFor homes and businesses. On commercial sites we locate the source with pressure tests.
    • New backflow prevention assembliesInstalled where a device is missing, required or past repair.
    • Results on fileFiled through the AquaResource portal, not by email or paper.
