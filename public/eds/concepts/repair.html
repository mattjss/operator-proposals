    Roof conditions guide

    What you're seeing, and what it means

    Three conditions Ed's sees most on Clark County roofs. If you can't tell whether to repair or replace, call for an inspection.

    1. 01Curling or buckling shingles

      What it means
      Shingles that lift at the edges or ripple no longer lie flat to shed water. In Ed's words: "When shingles curl they can cause serious structural damage."
      Repair or replace
      The crew checks how widespread it is. A roof that is past repair or at the end of its useful life is a candidate for a reroof; otherwise you get a repair.

    2. 02Granular loss

      What it means
      Asphalt shingles are coated with granules. Bare patches on the roof, or granules collecting in the gutters and downspouts, are a sign the shingles are wearing.
      Repair or replace
      The crew checks how much of the roof is affected and recommends a repair or a full replacement.

    3. 03Roof leakage

      What it means
      A stain on the ceiling, damp insulation in the attic or drips around a skylight or vent. The leak on the roof is often not directly above the stain.
      Repair or replace
      The crew finds where the water gets in and repairs it, or recommends a replacement if the roof is past repair. Delaying it can cost more later, so call when you notice it.
